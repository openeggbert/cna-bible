\chapter{glTF Importer, Runtime, and Test-Evidence Matrix}
\label{ch:gltf-evidence-matrix}

This matrix preserves three questions that a single ``supported'' column would collapse:
does the shared import core parse or transform the feature, does a CNA runtime route represent
and use it, and what executable evidence reaches that boundary?  It describes CNA at the pinned
revision.  ``None'' means no focused evidence was found, not proof of failure.  The detailed
coordinate, packing, animation, toolchain, and oracle explanations live in
Chapters~\ref{ch:gltf-import-core}--\ref{ch:gltf-conformance}.

\section{Route and oracle boundaries}

The direct reader and \texttt{cna\_tool\_gltf\_to\_cnj} share semantic extraction but do not
promise identical products.  The command-line route can emit every group and apply an explicit
unit scale; direct loading selects the first group and fixes scale at~1.  CNJ then serializes the
chosen model, buffers, materials and optional sidecars; a successful CNJ reload proves that
serialized boundary, not arbitrary parity with the original glTF file.

The conformance corpus implements five of seven planned oracle layers:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{clp{7.6cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Layer} & \textbf{State} & \textbf{Observation} \\
\midrule
L1 & implemented & source/corpus identity, container and high-level counts \\
L2 & implemented & accessor and decoded-value dumps \\
L3 & implemented & semantic mesh/material/animation digest \\
L4 & implemented & scene graph, bone hierarchy and composed transforms \\
L5 & implemented & packed GPU-buffer bytes and stride interpretation \\
L6 & absent & effect binding and complete draw-parameter observation \\
L7 & absent & rendered glTF pixel/golden comparison \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Consequently, the matrix can make strong claims through packed buffers and selected runtime
objects, and it can lock known wrong behavior without calling it correct.  It cannot promote
every successful import to material fidelity or visible rendering.  D1--D4 and D8 are fixed at
the pin; D5 is partially remediated by named rejection; D6 and D7 remain executable known
defects.  The ledger's bidirectional meta-test requires open defects to keep tests and fixed
defects to relinquish obsolete expected-failure locks.
