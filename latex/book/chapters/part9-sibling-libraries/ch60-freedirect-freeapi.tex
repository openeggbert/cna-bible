\chapter{free-direct and free-api}
\label{ch:freedirect-freeapi}
\label{ch:freedirect-deep-dive}

This book's FREEDIRECT renderer chapter covered how CNA fronts \texttt{free-direct}; this short
chapter covers \texttt{free-direct} itself, kept deliberately brief --- it is a peripheral
dependency for CNA, backing exactly one 2D-only renderer, not a project on the scale of
\texttt{sharp-runtime} or \texttt{easy-gl}.

\section{A narrow subset, defined by two real games}

\texttt{free-direct} does not aim at DirectX~3 compatibility in general. Its own scope is
defined entirely by what two specific legacy Windows games --- \emph{Speedy Blupi} (via the
sibling \texttt{free-eggbert} repository) and \emph{Planet Blupi} --- actually call, confirmed
by a real, reproducible call-site audit against both games' own source at named, dated
commits, not by guessing at what a typical DirectDraw-era game might need. Its own
\texttt{include/dplay.h}, for instance, defines only the DirectPlay error codes, flags, and
two interfaces (\texttt{IDirectPlay}, \texttt{IDirectPlay2A}) those two games' call sites
actually exercise --- there is no \texttt{IDirectPlay3A} at all, no group or lobby methods,
and even \texttt{DPID\_ALLPLAYERS}/\texttt{DPID\_SYSMSG} are absent, confirmed by direct grep
rather than assumed missing.

\section{Three subsystems, three different maturity levels}

\textbf{DirectDraw} is the most complete: primary and offscreen surface creation,
\texttt{Blt}/\texttt{BltFast} with clipping and source color-key handling, palette support,
a minimal clipper, and \texttt{Lock}/\texttt{Unlock} for direct pixel access, all real. Some
paths are deliberately minimal rather than incomplete by accident ---
\texttt{SetDisplayMode}, \texttt{IsLost}, and \texttt{Restore} are thin, matching what the
target games actually need rather than a full DirectDraw device-loss model.

\textbf{DirectSound} is partially real: SDL3-backed playback of static PCM buffers, real
volume and constant-power panning for mono sources, real lock/unlock semantics --- but
looping is accepted and silently ignored, and a playing buffer's position is not genuinely
seekable.

\textbf{DirectPlay} is the most surprising of the three, precisely because it is more real
than its narrow scope might suggest: genuine session hosting, joining, player management, and
unicast/broadcast message delivery all work between two \texttt{free-direct} processes, over
either a default loopback transport or a real, optional ENet-backed UDP transport with real
LAN host discovery --- explicitly \emph{FreeDirect-to-FreeDirect only}, never wire-compatible
with genuine Microsoft DirectPlay at the packet level. Seven previously-open DirectPlay design
questions (broadcast semantics, ENet host discovery, LAN discovery, host routing, player
names, duplicate-player detection, player-lost state) have all been asked of, and answered by,
the project owner and are now either implemented or deliberately left unimplemented by
explicit decision, not by default.

\section{A revealing implementation detail: DPID as a hazard}

One specific detail is worth naming as a small lesson in porting fidelity: \texttt{free-direct}
types its \cnaclass{DPID} player-identifier as a raw \texttt{DWORD\_PTR} rather than a stable,
opaque handle --- a genuine hazard flagged directly in the project's own call-site audit,
since code that assumes a \texttt{DPID} is a small, stable integer can behave unexpectedly if
that assumption is violated. This is exactly the kind of small representational choice this
book has flagged elsewhere (Chapter~\ref{ch:networking}'s \cnaclass{PacketReader}/%
\cnaclass{PacketWriter} \cnaclass{Color} asymmetry, for instance) as worth knowing about
before writing code against an API, even when it does not manifest as an outright bug in
either of the two games the library currently targets.

\section{Non-goals, restated}

Consistent with this book's coverage: no hardware-accurate emulation, no Direct3D pipeline at
all (matching the target games, which never used one), and no expansion of the supported API
surface beyond what \emph{Speedy Blupi} and \emph{Planet Blupi} actually call --- a third
game wanting to use \texttt{free-direct} would need its own call-site audit first, not an
assumption that the existing surface is already broad enough.
